# Audiences & Use Cases

## The qualifying question

> **"Do you regularly have a thinking problem you can't solve in a linear document or a chat window?"**

If yes, they're a ThinkingCanvas user. Their deliverable is a **decision, a strategy, or a structure, not a document**, and the thinking *is* the work.

---

## Primary audiences (launch ICPs)

### 1. Founders and early builders
- **Their thinking problem:** a vast idea space, high stakes, sudden clicks. Blank pages and chat windows capture none of the *trail*.
- **Jobs to be done:** find product-market fit (diverge, then converge) · stress-test the thesis *before* investors find the crack · catch the link between two hunches · keep the original problem in view as the company pivots around it.
- **Where ThinkingCanvas shines:** the immutable north star against drift ("are we still solving the problem we started with?"); Question edges between market observations; the Stress-Tester on the pitch logic *(when reachable)*.
- **Trigger moments:** pivot decisions, fundraising narrative, pricing, first hires, "why aren't users retaining?".
- **Watch-out:** founders are the most tempted by "just tell me the answer". Position ThinkingCanvas as the place where *their* conviction is formed, which investors can sense.

### 2. Product managers
- **Their thinking problem:** constant context switching between user problems, constraints, and goals; thinking scattered across tools.
- **Jobs to be done:** diverge on the problem before converging on a solution · find cross-domain analogies for hard UX problems (Outer Subconscious) · carry unresolved threads into the next sprint (carry-forward) · notice when a feature debate quietly became a different problem (drift).
- **Trigger moments:** discovery synthesis, roadmap trade-offs, PRD pre-thinking, post-mortems.
- **Watch-out:** PMs live in team tools, and ThinkingCanvas is single-user. Frame it as the *pre-work* before the team doc.

### 3. Researchers and academics
- **Their thinking problem:** synthesising literature and forming original arguments; linear notes destroy the structure.
- **Jobs to be done:** the directed graph *is* an argument's structure · the Articulator links papers they sensed were related · session-end open questions become the next session's start · contradictions surfaced between sources.
- **Trigger moments:** literature reviews, forming a thesis, grant framing, paper outlines.
- **Watch-out:** they'll want citations and sources. The Outer Subconscious draws on model knowledge and isn't grounded in retrieval, so never position it as a citation tool.

### 4. Consultants and strategists
- **Their thinking problem:** complex client problems, competing hypotheses, the need to synthesise fast and present with confidence.
- **Jobs to be done:** build the causal map of a situation · have the Stress-Tester challenge the recommendation before the client does · see what was left unresolved · prove *their own* reasoning (the trail is the audit).
- **Trigger moments:** issue trees, hypothesis-driven problem solving, workshop prep.
- **Watch-out:** they'll ask for export and sharing, which doesn't exist yet.

## Secondary audiences (high value, slightly later)

| Audience | Fit | What's missing first |
|---|---|---|
| **Writers and essayists** | Narrative and argument map naturally onto a graph; the Articulator names thematic links | Export to an outline |
| **Graduate students** | Thesis development over many sessions; carry-forward; drift from the research question | Price sensitivity; possible academic tier |
| **Coaches and therapists** | Mapping a client's thinking live | **Multiplayer / shared view**; privacy and compliance posture |
| **Executives** | Strategic decisions with high stakes and little time | Polished onboarding; the proactive AI working |
| **Learners / self-directed thinkers** | Understanding a hard topic by building it | A lighter-weight onboarding |

## Anti-personas (who we should *not* target)

- **"Just give me the answer" users**: those who want a report, an essay, or code written. The product will frustrate them, and they'll churn loudly. Point them to a chatbot.
- **Team-whiteboard buyers**: those who need real-time collaboration and sticky-note workshops (Miro/FigJam territory). ThinkingCanvas is single-user.
- **Note hoarders**: those who want a second brain for capturing everything. ThinkingCanvas is for working a *problem*, not storing knowledge.
- **Diagram makers**: flowcharts and architecture diagrams where the output is the picture.

## Use-case scenarios (for content, demos, and onboarding)

Each scenario marks which parts are **live** (✅) and which depend on the proactive AI (🟠).

### A. "Why is retention dropping after week 2?" (PM / founder), the canonical demo
- North star: *Why is our user retention dropping after week 2?*
- The user maps observations and draws a **Question** edge between "week-2 usage is solo" and "referral users retain better" ✅. The Outer Subconscious brings in how multiplayer games build retention through social obligation, and asks what the *social* first-week action would be.
- A **Relate** edge between "setup wizard" and "time to first value" ✅. The Articulator offers three readings: a causal chain, a trade-off, and a shared root cause.
- 🟠 An Expander reframe: *"Not fewer steps. You're moving the first 'it works' moment earlier."*
- "I'm done" ✅: the Observer flags drift from *retention* into *activation*, and asks whether that's evolution or drift.

### B. The pivot decision (founder)
- North star: *Should we pivot from SMB to mid-market?*
- Two sessions across a week. Session 1 diverges on signals ✅ (Question edges into the unknowns). Session 2 opens with carried-forward open questions ✅ and converges, with the Stress-Tester attacking the "mid-market has budget" assumption 🟠.
- Value: a traceable reason for the decision that the founder owns and can defend.

### C. Literature synthesis (researcher)
- North star: *What explains the replication failures in X?*
- Nodes per paper and claim. **Relate** edges between papers sensed to connect ✅, with 2–3 articulations to choose from. Contradictions between sources surface at Session Complete ✅.
- Caveat: the AI adds perspectives and names concepts; it isn't a source of citations.

### D. Client hypothesis tree (consultant)
- North star: *Why are Client Co.'s margins eroding?*
- Hypotheses as nodes, and a **Question** edge wherever a link is suspected but unproven ✅. Rejected ghosts ("too abstract") keep later suggestions concrete ✅.
- The Stress-Tester on the final recommendation 🟠 is the killer moment for this persona.

### E. Personal decision (anyone)
- North star: *Should I take the offer in Berlin?*
- A low-stakes on-ramp that shows the product's respect for ownership: the AI never tells you what to do; it asks the question you've been avoiding.

### F. Writing an essay's argument (writer)
- North star: *What's the actual argument of my essay on remote work?*
- Map claims; the Articulator names how two sections relate ✅; an appreciation ghost marks the real thesis when it appears 🟠.
